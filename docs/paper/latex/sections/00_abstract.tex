\begin{abstract}
Systematic reviews underpin parameterization of infectious disease models but are limited by labor-intensive screening of large literature corpora with low inclusion yield and by the downstream effort required to code and extract parameter evidence from full texts for quantitative synthesis. Although large language models (LLMs) show promise for automation, their performance in literature screening remains suboptimal without domain-specific design. Here, we present a LLM-assisted workflow for literature screening and full-text parameter coding and extraction, evaluating articles across three epidemiological characteristics: transmission potential, transmission speed, and disease severity. Using a benchmark of 22 COVID-19 and mpox source-review screening tasks (12,758 records; 668 included studies), the model achieves consistently high recall across parameters: 0.984 (95\% CI: 0.959--0.994) for reproduction number, 0.935 (95\% CI: 0.894--0.961) for serial interval, and 0.957 (95\% CI: 0.919--0.977) for fatality; with corresponding NNS values of 5.70 (original unassisted NNS, 14.13), 2.55 (14.11), and 7.47 (30.19), respectively. In a component-level full-text parameter coding and extraction evaluation using source-review included studies as inputs, LLM-coded epidemiological estimates closely matched source-review point estimates across 22 source reviews, with parameter-stratified MAEs of 0.190 days for serial interval, 0.165 for reproduction number, and 0.416 pp for fatality.
Together, these findings suggest that LLM-assisted workflows can reduce first-pass screening workload while retaining high sensitivity and assist full-text parameter coding and extraction as a modular component of evidence synthesis.

\end{abstract}
